Festival Cricket · Blaze Upgrade Guide

How to move the Firebase project to the paid Blaze plan for the match day only, then back to the free Spark plan. For the organiser (owner account).
Why: the free Spark plan allows 50,000 database reads a day. With hundreds of spectators following live matches, a busy day can use around 400,000. Blaze keeps the same free daily allowance and charges only for usage above it, roughly $0.20–$3 for a busy day. These are estimates; check current prices on the Firebase pricing page.

1The day before: upgrade to Blaze

  1. Open console.firebase.google.com and choose the festival project.
  2. At the bottom of the left menu, next to the plan name Spark, click Upgrade.
  3. Choose Blaze (pay as you go).
  4. Choose a billing account, or create one by entering a card. New Google Cloud billing accounts often include free trial credit, which may cover the whole day.
  5. When asked, set a budget (e.g. $10) and keep the email alerts on.
  6. Confirm. The upgrade is immediate; nothing in the apps needs to change.

2On match day: keep an eye on usage

  • Check Firestore Database → Usage during the day to see reads so far.
  • Watch your email for budget alerts.
  • If usage or costs climb too fast, open the cricket app → Settings → untick Public viewing → Save. Spectators see "Live scores are paused" and stop using reads; scorers carry on normally. Tick it again later to resume.

3After the festival: back to free Spark

  1. Firebase console → gear icon → Usage and billing → Details & settings.
  2. Click Modify plan and choose Spark (free). Confirm.
  3. Charges for the time on Blaze appear on the next monthly bill for that billing account.

Downgrading is safe: the sports and cricket apps only use Firestore and sign-in, which both work on Spark. No data is lost.
